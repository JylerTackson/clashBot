# Musketeer

> *In-game description:* "Don't be fooled by her delicately coiffed hair, the Musketeer is a mean shot with her trusty boomstick."

**Related:** [Evolution: musketeer-evolution](../evolutions/musketeer-evolution.md) · [Hero variant: musketeer-hero](../heroes/musketeer-hero.md)

## Overview

The Musketeer is a Rare card that is unlocked in the Training Camp. It spawns one single-target, air-targeting, long-ranged, and ground troop with moderate hitpoints and moderate damage. The Musketeer takes the appearance of a woman who has blue eyes and purple coiffed curly hair and eyebrows, and wears a steel blue tunic with a team-colored cloth on it, a team-colored cape, a metal helmet with a team-colored feather and a tiny crater on it, a belt, a metal shoulder pad, and brown boots, while carrying a blunderbuss-styled musket as her weapon with dark gray spheres as her ammunition. A Musketeer card costs 4 Elixir to deploy.

<!-- Card Overviews summary (wiki) -->
A consistent 4 Elixir single-target shot attacking both air and ground with fairly average stats — she is a reliable support or defense unit with a trusty boomstick that deals enough damage to take out small tanks but enough speed to quickly handle small swarms, though she's vulnerable to spells.

For 2 Cycles, if her **Evolution** is active, she'll gain a scope that lets her shoot enemies INFINITELY away from her vertically. A **Sniper Shot** gained from the improved and scope-induced boomstick can destroy all entities vulnerable to Arrows, while three Sniper Shots will take down the Archer Queen herself! She only has three of those Sniper Shots, though, and they can easily go to waste, so use her accordingly! Her Sniper Shots also don't work against Crown Towers.

If her **Hero** is active, you'll notice she has way more friends than her boomstick, because for 3 Elixir, you can use her **Trusty Turret** ability, summoning a fast-attacking, ground and air-targeting turret that has a fair share of hitpoints, but a short lifetime and below average range.
