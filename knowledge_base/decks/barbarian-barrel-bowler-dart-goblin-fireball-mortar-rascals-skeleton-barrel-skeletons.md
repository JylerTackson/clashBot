## Classification

Primary archetype: [bait](../archetypes/bait.md). Ryley calls it "a mortar bait deck" (352-357s) and it runs the classic small-spell bait shell — Evo Skeleton Barrel, Rascals, Skeletons, Dart Goblin — around a Mortar that he plays defensively far more often than as a siege engine.

## Why this deck works

The list pairs two chip win conditions the opponent must answer differently: a Mortar that doubles as the main defensive building, and an Evolution Skeleton Barrel whose skeletons and death bomb punish a used-up spell. Rascals, Skeletons, Dart Goblin and Barbarian Barrel form a dense low-cost defensive core, so the deck can absorb Royal Giant pushes almost indefinitely — in this game Ryley describes it as building "like a brick wall on defense" (541-543s). The Heroic Bowler is the flex piece and the real damage: he calls it "the only thing that gives him trouble" in the matchup (397-402s), uses cheap Skeletons to get the Stone Swish ability locked onto the tower for roughly 500 damage (408-412s), and treats a back-placed Royal Giant as a free Bowler in double/triple elixir (572-579s). Its weakness is spell exposure — Rascals, Mortar and Dart Goblin all sit near the tower, so a faster Fireball-cycle deck can chip it out (463-483s) — which the pilot answers with constant bridge pressure and by taking every free Fireball on the enemy tower.
