## Summary

The second duel shown from a CRL monthly-finals set (video 228.4s-524.5s), and this video is a tournament video where every game is a different list, so the deck here is the per-game read plus the commentary, not any video-level consensus. Ryley is on a 2.75-average Goblin Drill cycle deck — Goblin Drill, Cannon, Bomber, Hero Valkyrie, Goblins, Fire Spirit, Evo Giant Snowball and Poison — and he says why he brought it at 229-242s: "after that game one, I did decide to go for Goblin Drill. Not only is Goblin Drill a really comfortable deck for me, but I did think that it also would just be a solid deck choice. With him using Royal Giant Monk, I did feel like that I win against most of the cycle decks that he could end up playing." The opponent turns out to be on Evo Mortar with Goblinstein and Cannon Cart — Ryley calls it at 265-277s ("it right here is when I figured out he was actually playing with mortar... I feel like every CRL mortar just always ends up popping out and being super popular") — the same eight as the knowledge base's [EvoMortar Goblinstein Cart](../decks/barbarian-barrel-cannon-cart-elite-barbarians-fireball-goblinstein-minions-mortar-skeletons.md). The game is a control clinic rather than a race: Ryley never builds a big push, he drills the enemy right corner over and over, answers each Mortar/Cannon Cart/Elite Barbarians attempt with 1-4 elixir, and the enemy right tower falls 3052 -> 2337 (t=350) -> 1292 (t=390) -> 657 (t=488) -> 177 at the last readable frame while his own worst tower is still above 1600. Regular time ends 0-0 at t≈398 and the whole second half of the clip is overtime; he closes it out on defence — "I wasn't really worried at all knowing that I had the Evo snowball in hand. I just dropped my Evo snowball there, guys, took zero damage on defense, and I was able to secure my first duel" (505-512s). He notes at 512-521s that this put him "two to zero in the CRL, and I had to win five more to qualify."

## Lessons stated by Ryley

- 236-247s (clock 2:42-2:30): pick the deck against the opponent's *likely* deck, not just the one they last showed — "With him using Royal Giant Monk, I did feel like that I win against most of the cycle decks that he could end up playing. And I also thought that there was a decent chance... he could go with a bridge spam deck because, you know, I just used a spirit, Tesla Evo, furnace, log, all of that kind of stuff is really good against bridge spam."
- 269-273s (clock 2:08): "Mortar is another really popular CRL deck that you do always have to be careful for."
- 340-356s (clock 0:58-0:45): use the drill to drain, not just to damage — "I was about to go in for a Goblin Drill because I knew that he would have to spend a Cannon Cart... I forced out so much elixir with that goblin drill."
- 344-352s (clock 0:52): predict the swarm with the small spell instead of reacting to it — "I think I went for a prediction snowball on top of the skeletons. And yeah, I did actually hit the skeletons with the snowball and he panicked and dropped a fireball."
- 361-366s (clock 0:36-0:32): "I was doing a really good job at just keeping control of the game, just always making sure that I never really gave him a opportunity to actually get a good push going."
- 368-378s (clock 0:30-0:21): spend the ability to remove a threat rather than re-defending it next cycle — "I went for the Valkyrie ability just to make sure that I take out that cannon cart cuz otherwise... I would have had to defend it again. So, I would have just rather spend elixir, take it off the board, and just keep control."
- 382-389s (clock 0:16-0:09): hold the cheap spell for the full value instead of casting on the first target — "I knew he would minions, but then I realized I could just wait and snowball literally everything. I got a beautiful snowball right there getting me a crazy amount of damage on the tower."
- 390-399s (overtime): spending a big spell defensively is fine when it removes a recurring problem — "I had to go for a defensive poison cuz I didn't want to have to worry about that goblin stein and the evil [evo] mortar anymore... It's just always good to poison that thing off the board and play it safe."
- 402-412s (overtime): when you are ahead on damage, mirror their plays from the back and force them to attack into you — "I'm always just trying to make sure that I'm mirroring his plays, but always having him going into me. That's why it's nice that I'm up in damage cuz he has to go into me. There's no um other way about it for him to do anything."
- 456-462s (overtime): protect the fragile splash unit from the fast melee card — "I was just making sure to block my bomber from getting taken out by the e-barb spear cuz it's really easy to choke against e-barbs."
- 465-482s (overtime): a missed prediction still has value if it preserves your other answer, and you do not have to spend a spell just because the value is there — "even though I didn't hit anything with the cannon, right? It just put me in a good spot to get value off of like a poison... here, look, another crazy potential poison. I didn't take it this time because I didn't think that I would need it."
- 500-512s (overtime): when ahead, define the win condition as not losing a tower — "all I knew all I had to do here was just not lose my tower, and everything would be okay... I wasn't really worried at all knowing that I had the Evo snowball in hand."
