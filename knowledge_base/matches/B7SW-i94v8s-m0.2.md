## Lessons stated by Ryley

- 551-557s: pressure is the air defence. "This is going to be another match where we have to apply super good pressure to be able to defend, otherwise we will lose because we don't really have the greatest air defense."
- 566-572s: punish a slow opening. "I want to be really aggressive right now because of that play that he made with the balloon in the back."
- 593-610s: a one-tower trade is fine for this deck. "Even if I get in a one-to-one tower situation, it's still very winnable... I just need to keep on applying pressure... My pushes are just kind of like consistent and cheap, but he makes fewer attacks that just do so much damage."
- 624-628s: cycling a defensive card tells the opponent when to commit. "Now that we've cycled the marcher, he's just going to go for a [Miner] balloon and try to take out my entire tower."
- 638s: Wall Breakers are a defensive tool as well as a win condition. "I'm definitely going to go for the wall breakers to kite, as well."
- 641s: hold Royal Delivery to the last possible moment of a defence. "We can go for the delivery last second, as well."
- 673-675s: place the barrel where their answer is going to go. "Let's barrel here in the hunter placement, cuz if he doesn't see it, then we'll get a bunch of damage when he goes for the hunter."
- 683-687s: finish the tower you have already committed to. "I'm going to march at the bridge, just to make sure that we finish off this tower, cuz if I don't finish off this tower, I will just lose."
- 692-698s: give up a tower you were losing anyway. "I think I have to let this tower go, guys... I think doing that just saves me from losing both sides, actually."
- 708-711s: do not spend the evolution into a push. "Low-key, I don't want to yet. I think it's too risky to go for the wall breakers right now."
