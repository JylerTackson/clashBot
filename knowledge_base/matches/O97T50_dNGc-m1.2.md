## Summary

A Clash Royale League Monthly Finals set against a named opponent, Mateja (968s: "In the next duel here, guys, we were up against Mateja"). Ryley opens the duel on a Battle Ram bridge-spam list — Battle Ram, Elite Barbarians, Cannon Cart, Hero Valkyrie, Mother Witch, Zappies, Heal Spirit and Arrows — and says why: "I decided to start off with this bridge spam deck here. I just thought that it would be a little bit unpredictable because I hadn't really used any bridge spam up until this point in the tournament. I also thought that he could start off with mortar bait against me and I did have a pretty high feeling that I would beat mortar bait with this bridge spam deck" (969-987s). The read is wrong: "I was very, very wrong about him having mortar bait. Um, he ended up having a giant skeleton executioner deck" (988-995s), with a Bomb Tower, Miner, Zappies and Arrows behind it, and he calls that matchup "so bad for me" because of the Evolution Executioner (1044-1047s).

He wins it anyway by never letting the opponent bank elixir. Regular time is a single long bridge-spam grind: Elite Barbarians into the right bridge behind a surviving Cannon Cart while a Battle Ram goes down the opposite lane (1010-1015s), which strips the enemy left tower from 3052 to 806 HP inside twenty seconds. From clock 1:20 onwards it is all defence-into-counterpush: a Mother Witch cursing the Giant Skeleton, Hero Valkyrie's Wild Whirlwind used repeatedly as a dash into the tower, Cannon Cart and Elite Barbarians dropped "in the pocket" on the opponent's half, and Heal Spirits on top of everything. Neither crown tower ever falls: overtime ends with the enemy pair at 455 and 135 HP against Ryley's 2764 / 3052 (his king was chipped to 2836), so the game is decided on the tiebreaker. Ryley states the win explicitly at 1211-1214s — "I was able to win this really really unfortunate match-up and we were going on to game two."

## Opponent

Mateja's deck is what Ryley calls "a giant skeleton executioner deck" (993-995s), and the observed cards are consistent with that: an **[Executioner](../cards/executioner.md)** (Evolution — Ryley calls it "Evo Executioner") tracked at (13, 19), (13, 13), (15, 21) and repeatedly down the right lane late; a **[Giant Skeleton](../cards/giant-skeleton.md)** at (2, 20) at t=1066.7 and again in the right lane at t=1112-1116; a **[Bomb Tower](../cards/bomb-tower.md)** placed four times at (8-10, 18-19); **[Arrows](../cards/arrows.md)** on the opponent's own half at (13, 23), (11, 23) and (12, 19) to clear Ryley's Cursed Hogs; plus **[Zappies](../cards/zappies.md)** ("He was able to get the zappies down", 1155s) and a **[Miner](../cards/miner.md)** referenced at 1031-1033s and again at 1166s.

How Ryley reads and counters it:

- He counts the opponent out of answers before committing: "I went for the cannon cart at the bridge knowing he had no zappies, no miner, no elixir for giant skelly" (1029-1034s).
- He treats the Evolution Executioner as the matchup-defining card and uses the Cannon Cart to force it out early: "getting out the Evo Executioner is huge for me knowing that it is really impossible for me to kill. That's kind of the card that makes this match up so bad for me ... he'd have to play two more just to get to the Evo" (1040-1052s).
- He exploits the deck's cost: "everything in his deck is really, really expensive" (1019-1022s), which is why bridge spam guarantees damage.
- He answers the Giant Skeleton with Mother Witch (curse -> Cursed Hog, t=1079.7) and with Heal Spirit splash (t=1141.4), never with a hard counter.
- Against the Executioner + Bomb Tower defensive core he uses Cursed Hogs as distraction rather than trying to kill them (1171-1177s).
- He predicts the opponent's finisher and pre-empts it in the opposite lane: "I knew he was going to go for the Giant Skelly [Balloon](../cards/balloon.md) so I E-Barbs the bridge opposite lane" (1104-1106s).

## Lessons stated by Ryley

- 972-987s — Deck choice in a tournament set is partly about being unread: "I decided to start off with this bridge spam deck here. I just thought that it would be a little bit unpredictable because I hadn't really used any bridge spam up until this point in the tournament."
- 1017-1022s — Bridge spam is the correct plan against an expensive deck: "I knew that right now I was guaranteed to get a ton of damage because obviously everything in his deck is really, really expensive."
- 1029-1036s — Commit at the bridge only after counting the opponent's cycle: "I went for the cannon cart at the bridge knowing he had no zappies, no miner, no elixir for giant skelly. So he had to evo executioner on my Cannon Cart."
- 1040-1052s — Forcing an Evolution out of rotation is worth an elixir trade: "getting out the Evo Executioner is huge ... he'd have to play two more just to get to the Evo."
- 1074-1079s — On the misplayed Elite Barbarians: the correct version was the opposite lane, "so the Giant Skelly wouldn't help clean them up."
- 1104-1117s — Take the damage while you can, or you lose the ability to close: "just making sure that I would take his left side tower cuz if I didn't I wouldn't be able to take his tower at all and I would just get completely cooked because he would just save his tower with a few hundred HP."
- 1135-1141s — Never release pressure in overtime: "I had to keep up the pressure because if I just let go then he would just build up a massive push [and] win the game."
- 1157-1164s — The goal of relentless multi-angle chip is forcing mandatory responses: "it was at the point where like he couldn't ignore any damage. He had to respond to everything. So I knew that it would be extremely hard for him to be able to win."
- 1171-1177s — Cursed Hogs as a defence disruptor: "the piggies really disrupt the defense cuz he has an executioner and a bomb tower ... the piggies are really good at distracting that."
