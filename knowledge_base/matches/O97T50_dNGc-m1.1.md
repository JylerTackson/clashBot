## Summary

Tournament video, so the deck changes game to game; here (video 779.5s-968.6s, the readable 189 s of a three-minute game plus overtime) Ryley is on WB Log Bait 2.8 — Wall Breakers, Evo Goblin Barrel, Princess, Dart Goblin, Evo Skeleton Army, Cannon, Ice Spirit and Hero Valkyrie. He names it himself at 780-785s: "now we whipped out our first log bait of the CRL. I was happy to be playing log bait man cuz I hadn't played log bait in a while for CRL." He opens misreading the opponent as a Giant/Sparky list, then corrects at 802-809s once the Zappies land: "Hold on, he's not playing Sparky... I kind of figured it out that he had bridge spam" — Evo Battle Ram, Evo Elite Barbarians, Hero Wizard, Giant Skeleton, Zappies, Arrows and Vines, a matchup he calls "pretty tough" because arrows-plus-vines answers every bait card he owns. The middle of the game goes badly: a Giant Skeleton push with the Hero Wizard behind it takes his left tower from 2668 down to 521 between clock 1:33 and 1:02 ("look at that Wizard, guys. That Wizard was just getting so so much damage"). It ends in overtime with one more Barrel + Wall Breakers: "look at that. The wall breakers somehow connected and I won the game for a very nice two to zero sweep."

## Opponent

Ryley's read at 802-838s, made off the Zappies rather than off any single tank: bridge spam with Evo Battle Ram, Evo Elite Barbarians, Hero Wizard, Giant Skeleton, Zappies, Arrows and Vines. The pipeline corroborates Zappies (arena detections at clock 2:09, 0:51, 0:47, 0:15 and repeatedly in overtime), Giant Skeleton (clock 1:33, 0:41, 0:07), Elite Barbarians and Battle Ram (detections at clock 0:34 and in overtime, matching "take out the e-barbs with my Valkyrie" and "the skarmy actually ended up pushing the ram").

How he plays around it:
- He never tries to out-defend it. The thesis stated at 876-881s is that the opponent's only two spells are Arrows and Vines and both are needed on defence, so the plan is to make every one of his own baits cost a spell: barrel forces arrows, Skarmy forces arrows, and Vines cannot clear Skarmy at all.
- Against the Giant Skeleton he stacks Skarmy + Cannon + Dart Goblin (clock 1:28-1:22) and accepts the Hero Wizard's chip as the price — his left tower goes to 521 but never dies, because Arrows and Vines cannot finish it.
- The Evo Battle Ram is handled with a held Ice Spirit rather than a building.
- He deliberately concedes spell value on offence when the trade is elixir-positive: "I didn't mind giving him like a little bit of value because I knew that in the end he was going to have to spend a bunch of elixir" (927-932s).

## Lessons stated by Ryley

- 802-809s: read the deck from the cheap support, not the win condition — "once I saw those Zappies, I'm like, 'Hold on, he's not playing Sparky.'"
- 815-828s: know which of your bait cards a matchup answers before you commit — "with arrows and vines... he's able to kill my Skarmy, my Dart Goblin, my Princess. Like, he's able to take out everything."
- 840-848s: pre-emptive pressure is correct when you know you cannot defend the counter-push — "I went for the Valkyrie Wall Breakers here just to apply some pressure, knowing that I was going to get like hit with a mean counter push... just normally I can't really defend."
- 876-881s: an opponent whose only spells are needed on defence cannot close out a low tower — "his only spells are arrows and vines, so he isn't really able to finish off my tower that well, especially when he needs to use the arrows and the vines on defense all the time."
- 895-907s: count what one spell can answer — "if I went for the Skeleton Army, he would have to arrows... He can only kill one of those three, right? Dart Goblin, Princess, or a Skarmy with the vines. He can't really kill Skarmy with vines."
- 916-932s: forcing a spell out is worth conceding some value — "that's where making him use the arrows helps me defend. Even though he got a good value, it still really helped me... I knew that in the end he was going to have to spend a bunch of elixir."
- 935-940s: on his own Wall Breakers timing — "I think I waited on the wall breakers, which was bad for me. I should have just instantly went."
