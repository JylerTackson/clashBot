## Summary

Game 3 of a six-game deck-showcase video in which Ryley voices over his own CRL qualifier matches; this segment is the first duel of the day against Sandbox — "now we were in the next duel against Sandbox, who is one of the best players in the world" (t=1074-1079) — with qualification on the line: "if I won this duel right here, I was guaranteed to qualify for the monthly final" (t=1098-1103). Ryley (bottom side) is on a 2.6-elixir Rocket cycle list — Berserker (Hero variant), Evo Cannon, Ice Spirit, Skeletons, Evo Princess, The Log, Royal Delivery, Rocket — and names the plan immediately: "I was really confident with this Rocket Cycle deck here. I just felt like any matchup I trust my defense, I can win" (t=1081-1087). Sandbox plays bridge spam (Battle Ram, Giant Skeleton, Mother Witch, Zappies, Wizard, Barbarian Barrel, Royal Hogs and an "ebar"), which Ryley rates as the hardest possible pairing — "Sandbox is one of, if not the best bridge spam player in the world" (t=1161-1165) — while still liking his own side of it.

For the whole readable window the defence holds. Evo Cannon is the spine, replaced roughly every 20-25 s (t=1091, 1167, 1191, 1225.6, 1251, 1261, 1283) with Ice Spirit, Skeletons, Berserker and The Log layered on top; the Evo Princess only goes to the bridge when a Cannon is already banked. The peak is t=1225-1242, where he answers an evolved Battle Ram and evolved Barbarians in opposite lanes down to 1 elixir for ~50 HP of tower damage — "Was taking absolutely no damage, but I was down five elixir" (t=1240-1244). He loses the damage lead to a Royal Hogs chip at t=1272-1274, takes it back by t=1288, and by t=1310 says "All I had to do was defend for another 50 seconds and this game would be mine."

The segment then cuts off mid-collapse: at t=1324-1337 he narrates declining a Rocket on a back-placed Giant Skeleton ("I got scared of rocketing, man. I just should have rocketed"), which is where the game turns. The rest of the same commentary continues past this segment's split boundary (the analysis window ends at t=1338.2 on a tower reset) and states the result: "I actually lost because of that, man... And I lost with a good matchup. So, I was 0 to 1" (t=1362-1377, in the game_4 segment of the same video). The same duel is also documented from the compilation upload as [9tx2iYsSmMw-m0.8](9tx2iYsSmMw-m0.8.md), where the ending is inside the window.

## Lessons stated by Ryley

- t=1081-1087 — a cheap all-defence cycle list is a matchup-agnostic pick if you trust your defence: "I was really confident with this Rocket Cycle deck here. I just felt like any matchup I trust my defense, I can win."
- t=1114-1122 — on competitive mindset: "you never want to like put your fate in other people's hands. I'd rather just put it in mine and trust myself, like trust my practice, trust my skill."
- t=1126-1133 — "there's no point to sulk about it cuz there still is a chance. But of course, you always want to do it off of your own merit."
- t=1142-1147 — spending a small spell to deny early chip is correct even when it is the opponent doing it: "It was a smart play by him. They're just making sure to take no princess damage early on."
- t=1175-1186 — the standard he holds himself to: "if I play 100% perfect, I should win this game... you're always trying to play perfect in this game. Like even if it doesn't happen very often, that's still what you like strive for."
- t=1195-1207 — bank a defensive building before you chip: "because I had a free cannon on the board, I also knew I could princess the bridge... it's still good to make these plays in my opinion. Just trying to get some, you know, slick damage on the tower here and there."
- t=1212-1224 — spend the big spell on defence rather than let the hand jam: "I knew that this might be a little bit risky, but I did think that I would be able to defend, but I also knew that like sometimes I need to do plays like that otherwise my hand is just going to be way too awkward to try and defend here."
- t=1245-1259 — track the opponent's Evolutions, not just their cards: "he had both of his evos right there. So since he had both of his evolutions, every other time I do that, it won't be as difficult to defend because the other cards will be a little bit weaker. So that was a important thing to keep in mind for like the future."
