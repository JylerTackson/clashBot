# Match: Minion Giant is the Most OVERPOWERED Card!! — game 4

## Summary

Last game of a deck-showcase video in which every game uses a different deck built around the new **Minion
Giant**, so there is no video-level deck to fall back on. Ryley announces this one himself at t=677-707: "a
really strange deck, but I really thought that this deck could work... I am playing with royal giant, but
instead of the royal giant, of course, I have the [Minion] Giant... I played with the monk because I want monk
in here. I feel like monk plus [minion] giant is going to be a really difficult combo to stop... And since
obviously fisherman is no longer needed because I don't have royal giant, I thought I should just add a
cannon as a little distraction." What the deploy labels confirm is the [Royal Giant](../cards/royal-giant.md)
cycle shell with three swaps: [Barbarian Barrel](../cards/barbarian-barrel.md),
[Electro Spirit](../cards/electro-spirit.md), [Fireball](../cards/fireball.md),
[Hunter](../cards/hunter.md) (evolution — he asks it to "net it" at t=874),
[Skeletons](../cards/skeletons.md), plus [Cannon](../cards/cannon.md) in the Fisherman slot and
[Monk](../cards/monk.md) in the Royal Ghost slot; the eighth card is the Minion Giant, which has no knowledge
base entry and is invisible to the perception pipeline. The opponent is on a Mortar siege list —
[Mortar](../cards/mortar.md), [Graveyard](../cards/graveyard.md), [Rascals](../cards/rascals.md),
[Cannon Cart](../cards/cannon-cart.md), [Hunter](../cards/hunter.md),
[Skeletons](../cards/skeletons.md), [Bomber](../cards/bomber.md), a
[Skeleton King](../cards/skeleton-king.md) skill — and, by Ryley's own account at t=820, a Minion Giant of
his own. The game is a grind: Mortar shells chip his tower all through single elixir, he trades the enemy
right tower down to 2305 with a right-lane Monk push at clock 1:35, swings to the left lane at clock 0:48 and
takes the enemy left tower from 2632 to 1380, and nobody crowns in regular time. In overtime he repeats
Monk-at-the-bridge plus Minion Giant behind it every rotation while Hunter and Skeletons hold the Mortar, and
the enemy left tower falls from 1380 to 128 before he calls it at t=941: "He has no mortar in cycle. This game
is over."

## How Ryley uses his cards

- **Minion Giant** (not in the knowledge base; the 8th deck slot). The whole deck is built to walk it in
  behind a Monk: "monk plus [minion] giant is going to be a really difficult combo to stop" (t=692-695), "as
  long as I keep just going monk and minion giant, I should be able to win this game" (t=725-730), "I can just
  put my monk towards his mortar and then I can just minion giant behind it every single time" (t=795-798). He
  plays it as a bridge threat, not from the back, and repeats it every rotation in overtime (t=744, 798, 866,
  879, 912, 933, 940). Two facts he states about it: [Fireball](../cards/fireball.md) and
  [Giant Snowball](../cards/giant-snowball.md) do not knock it back (t=851-858), and its range is what makes
  it strong — "he gives like spirit empress vibes in terms of the range" (t=717-720).
- **[Monk](../cards/monk.md)** — the tank in front of the Minion Giant and the reason the pair works. Deploy
  labels at [9, 10] (clock 1:50), [8, 9] (clock 0:57), [8, 1] (clock 0:32), [3, 10] and [2, 13] (overtime), so
  both back-of-court builds and bridge drops. He pops Pensive Protection constantly and says why: "most of the
  cards that take this out... like musketeer, even Tesla, right? You just pop the ability and you just tank
  for it for so long" (t=731-741). He also uses the ability defensively and pre-emptively — "Let's pop the
  ability early" (t=884) and "I want to go for the ability here just to make sure that we take this out and
  keep our hunter hopefully alive" (t=926-930). The failure case is stated too: at clock 1:35 the opponent's
  Rascals stop targeting him — "the rascals aren't getting targeted. Uh they aren't targeting the monk
  anymore. Unfortunately, we still got a lot of damage, but we definitely could have gotten more" (t=751-757).
- **[Hunter](../cards/hunter.md)** (evolution) — his defensive anchor and, in his words, the answer to the new
  card: "I think evo hunter is the best counter that I have found to this card so far. Of course, you're only
  going to be able to hunter like every third one... but it's still very strong though in general. Having that
  full counter every single time, you get like a little safety net" (t=768-783). Placed on his own half in
  front of the threat, never at the bridge: [3, 12] (clock 2:41), [2, 9] (clock 1:26), [6, 10] (clock 0:22),
  [1, 11] (clock 0:03), [1, 6] (OT 1:11). He calls the evolution net by name at t=874: "Hunter net it, please.
  Put in the net."
- **[Cannon](../cards/cannon.md)** — the Fisherman replacement, used purely as a distraction building rather
  than a primary defence: "I thought I should just add a cannon as a little distraction" (t=700-703), "I have
  to drop a cannon here just to distract" (t=713), "Let's go for the cannon here to distract and we go for the
  hunter" (t=824), "Let's cannon here low just to bring this guy over" (t=897-898). Deploy labels at [7, 12],
  [4, 12], [7, 10], [10, 11], [8, 13], [6, 10] — mid-court, pulling units off the lane. He also credits it

- **[Fireball](../cards/fireball.md)** — both push support and the closer. Deploy labels at [3, 21], [2, 23],
